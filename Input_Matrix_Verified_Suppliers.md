## What the supplier data changes (and confirms)

The verified counts sharpen three points and change none of the verdicts. **They confirm the two genuine hardware chokepoints are real but narrow:** the DMD is not merely "near single-source" — Texas Instruments is the *sole commercial manufacturer* — and the CMOS electrode array is foundry-gated; yet both are swamped by their host markets (projectors, all of electronics), so neither is a monitorable lever. **They confirm the reagents are uniformly un-controllable:** every cycle reagent, solvent, support, and enzyme has multiple independent suppliers, and the one apparent exception — acetic anhydride, a DEA List II precursor — is controlled for narcotics reasons, freely available to labs, and omitted from the OpenIDS build anyway. **And they add one nuance for supply-resilience (not misuse):** acetonitrile is ~90% a byproduct of acrylonitrile, so its supply is inelastic and shortage-prone — but a demonstrated GRAS substitute (propylene carbonate) removes even that friction for DIY synthesis. The only genuinely specialty *reagent* is the photolabile amidite class, from a handful of suppliers — narrow, method-specific, and still not a monitorable chokepoint.

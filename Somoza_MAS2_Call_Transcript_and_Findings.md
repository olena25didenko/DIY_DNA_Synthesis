## Part B — Mark's answers, mapped to your interview questions

- **Error rate / usable length:** ~**5% deletion per nucleotide** (highest of the high-throughput methods). Yield falls exponentially with length; 150-mers possible but with non-measurable correct-oligo fraction. Realistic strategy = make many short (~30-mer) oligos at ~5% correct yield, filter, then concatenate/assemble. → Confirms your "library-grade, error-prone; not gene-assembly-ready."
- **Build difficulty / who gets stuck:** **Nobody has independently built one yet.** All CAD/protocols on ChemRxiv. Hardest step is **optical alignment** ("an art") + the gluing/handling of optics; needs genuine optics expertise plus separate nucleic-acid chemistry skill. First build took **months**, now down to **a few weeks**. A **Korean group** (PhD student visited autumn 2025) may attempt it. "Difficult — probably impossible — outside a research environment."
- **Cost (this is the big one — see Part C):** **Optical parts alone ≈ €150,000**; system + nucleic-acid synthesizer ≈ €20,000; realistic **end-to-end ≈ €200,000–300,000** (incl. gases, regulators, climate-controlled room, lab space).
- **Who bought it / published use:** Ghent University (spatial transcriptomics); GSK (therapeutic RNA modifications); Somoza's own group (data storage, spatial transcriptomics, CRISPR guide-RNA modifications); a cancer-tissue spatial group of biologists (got it working); a company that tried **gene synthesis and was not successful.** Only publication of note is in *Nucleic Acids Research*. **No one uses it for gene/virus synthesis.**
- **Value proposition:** not cheap DNA — it's **flexibility**: large-scale RNA and non-natural modifications (2′-F, 2′-OMe, phosphorothioate, 5mC, 5hmC, abasic, dU) that are **not available commercially**.
- **Forensic signatures (developer's own view):** (1) **high deletion rate**; (2) likely **photo-induced damage** (uses UV light; low rate, still characterising via mass spec); (3) **lower depurination** because it uses **no acidic deblock** (light-based deprotection instead of acid). Suggests buying pools from different manufacturers, sequencing + mass spec, to build per-manufacturer fingerprints. → Directly supports your Chapter 4 approach, and he confirmed it unprompted.
